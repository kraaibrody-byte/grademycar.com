How we grade

No opinions. Just what owners report.

Every grade comes from complaints real owners filed with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration.

  1. 1

    Collect every complaint

    We pull every owner complaint filed with NHTSA for each model year, and count each one once, even when it's filed under several trim names.

  2. 2

    Make it fair

    Complaints are divided by roughly how many cars were sold, then each car is compared only with cars from the same model year.

  3. 3

    Grade it A to F

    You get engine, transmission and overall grades, plus the problem owners report most, and whether a recall covers it.

Questions

Good questions to ask before you trust a grade.

Is it really free?

Yes. Grading any car is free with no account: engine, transmission and overall grades, plus the #1 problem owners report. The premium report (from $1.99) adds every reported problem with what to check, a deal score, miles remaining and full recall data.

Where does the data come from?

From complaints owners file with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), the federal agency that tracks vehicle defects and runs recalls. We've analyzed more than 662,000 of them across 120 popular models and every model year from 2005 to 2024.

Does a bad grade mean the car I'm looking at is bad?

Not necessarily. A grade describes how that model year did across thousands of owners. A well-maintained car from a weak year can be fine, especially if recall repairs were done. Use the grade to know what to check, and always get a pre-purchase inspection.

What if my car is older than 2005 or brand new?

You'll get the model's overall grade across its recent years instead of a year-specific one. Brand-new model years haven't collected many complaints yet, so we label those grades as early.
