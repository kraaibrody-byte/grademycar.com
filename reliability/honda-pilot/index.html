Honda Pilot reliability FAQ

Is the Honda Pilot reliable?

Average reliability. The Honda Pilot earns a C+ overall reliability grade from GradeMyCar, with a C for its engine and a C+ for its transmission. It ranks 10th of 23 midsize SUVs we grade. Its engine (C) and transmission (C+) grade about the same, so neither stands out as a weak point. Grades are based on 5,716 owner complaints filed with NHTSA for the 2013-2023 model years, adjusted for how many were sold.

How many miles will a Honda Pilot last?

A well-maintained Honda Pilot typically lasts around 260,000 miles, and owners have reported examples reaching 350,000 miles. About 20% reach 250,000 miles.

What are the best years for the Honda Pilot?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the most reliable Honda Pilot model years are 2007, 2008, 2010, 2014 and 2015.

What Honda Pilot years should I avoid?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the Honda Pilot years to be most careful with are 2016, 2017 and 2019. The 2016 grades an F overall (engine F, transmission D), and its most common complaint is stalling / loss of power, which a recall covers. The 2017 grades a D overall (engine D, transmission D), and its most common complaint is stalling / loss of power, which a recall covers.

Is the Honda Pilot transmission reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Honda Pilot transmission a C+ (average reliability). Reliability varies by model year, so grade the specific year you're looking at.

Is the Honda Pilot engine reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Honda Pilot engine a C (below-average reliability).

Grades are estimates for a make and model across model years, not a specific vehicle. Always get a pre-purchase inspection and check open recalls by VIN.
