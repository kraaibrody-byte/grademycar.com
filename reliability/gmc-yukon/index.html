GMC Yukon reliability FAQ

Is the GMC Yukon reliable?

Below-average reliability. The GMC Yukon earns a C- overall reliability grade from GradeMyCar, with a C for its engine and a C- for its transmission. It ranks 5th of 6 full-size SUVs we grade. Both its engine (C) and transmission (C-) grade poorly, so a pre-purchase inspection by a mechanic is a must. Grades are based on 3,838 owner complaints filed with NHTSA for the 2013-2023 model years, adjusted for how many were sold.

How many miles will a GMC Yukon last?

A well-maintained GMC Yukon typically lasts around 270,000 miles, and owners have reported examples reaching 380,000 miles. About 19.4% reach 250,000 miles.

What are the best years for the GMC Yukon?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the most reliable GMC Yukon model years are 2006, 2012, 2013 and 2014.

What GMC Yukon years should I avoid?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the GMC Yukon years to be most careful with are 2015, 2021, 2022 and 2023. The 2015 grades an F overall (engine C, transmission D), and its top engine/transmission problem is transmission slipping / rough shifting, which a recall covers. The 2021 grades an F overall (engine F, transmission F), and its top engine/transmission problem is engine failure / knocking, which a recall covers.

Is the GMC Yukon transmission reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the GMC Yukon transmission a C- (below-average reliability). Reliability varies by model year, so grade the specific year you're looking at.

Is the GMC Yukon engine reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the GMC Yukon engine a C (below-average reliability).

Grades are estimates for a make and model across model years, not a specific vehicle. Always get a pre-purchase inspection and check open recalls by VIN.
