Hyundai Santa Fe reliability FAQ

Is the Hyundai Santa Fe reliable?

Poor reliability. The Hyundai Santa Fe earns a D overall reliability grade from GradeMyCar, with a D for its engine and a C- for its transmission. It ranks 19th of 23 midsize SUVs we grade. The engine (D) is the weaker of its two major components, so ask for oil-change records and have a mechanic check for leaks and oil consumption. Grades are based on 5,033 owner complaints filed with NHTSA for the 2013-2023 model years, adjusted for how many were sold.

How many miles will a Hyundai Santa Fe last?

A well-maintained Hyundai Santa Fe typically lasts around 190,000 miles, and owners have reported examples reaching 250,000 miles. About 8.9% reach 250,000 miles.

What are the best years for the Hyundai Santa Fe?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the most reliable Hyundai Santa Fe model years are 2005 and 2006.

What Hyundai Santa Fe years should I avoid?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the Hyundai Santa Fe years to be most careful with are 2009, 2017, 2022 and 2023. The 2017 grades an F overall (engine F, transmission F), and its top engine/transmission problem is engine failure / knocking. The 2009 grades a D overall (engine D, transmission C+), and its top engine/transmission problem is stalling / loss of power.

Is the Hyundai Santa Fe transmission reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Hyundai Santa Fe transmission a C- (below-average reliability). Reliability varies by model year, so grade the specific year you're looking at.

Is the Hyundai Santa Fe engine reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Hyundai Santa Fe engine a D (poor reliability).
