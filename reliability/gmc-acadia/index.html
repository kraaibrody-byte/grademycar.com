GMC Acadia reliability FAQ

Is the GMC Acadia reliable?

Average reliability. The GMC Acadia earns a B- overall reliability grade from GradeMyCar, with a B for its engine and a C for its transmission. It ranks 7th of 23 midsize SUVs we grade. The transmission (C) is the weaker of its two major components, so ask for transmission service records and pay close attention to shifting on a test drive. Grades are based on 2,940 owner complaints filed with NHTSA for the 2013-2023 model years, adjusted for how many were sold.

How many miles will a GMC Acadia last?

A well-maintained GMC Acadia typically lasts around 160,000 miles, and owners have reported examples reaching 220,000 miles. About 7.7% reach 250,000 miles.

What are the best years for the GMC Acadia?

No GMC Acadia model year stands out as exceptionally reliable in NHTSA owner complaints. The best-graded years are 2013, 2016 and 2022.

What GMC Acadia years should I avoid?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the GMC Acadia years to be most careful with are 2008, 2009 and 2017. The 2008 grades an F overall (engine D, transmission F), and its top engine/transmission problem is transmission slipping / rough shifting. The 2009 grades a D overall (engine C-, transmission D), and its top engine/transmission problem is transmission slipping / rough shifting, which a recall covers.

Is the GMC Acadia transmission reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the GMC Acadia transmission a C (below-average reliability). Reliability varies by model year, so grade the specific year you're looking at.

Is the GMC Acadia engine reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the GMC Acadia engine a B (above-average reliability).

Grades are estimates for a make and model across model years, not a specific vehicle. Always get a pre-purchase inspection and check open recalls by VIN.
