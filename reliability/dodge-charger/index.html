Dodge Charger reliability FAQ

Is the Dodge Charger reliable?

Excellent reliability. The Dodge Charger earns an A- overall reliability grade from GradeMyCar, with an A- for its engine and a B+ for its transmission. It ranks 1st of 5 sports cars we grade. Its engine (A-) and transmission (B+) grade about the same, so neither stands out as a weak point. Grades are based on 1,525 owner complaints filed with NHTSA for the 2013-2023 model years, adjusted for how many were sold.

How many miles will a Dodge Charger last?

A well-maintained Dodge Charger typically lasts around 170,000 miles, and owners have reported examples reaching 240,000 miles. About 8.6% reach 250,000 miles.

What are the best years for the Dodge Charger?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the most reliable Dodge Charger model years are 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021 and 2022.

What Dodge Charger years should I avoid?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the Dodge Charger years to be most careful with are 2006, 2007 and 2012. The 2006 grades an F overall (engine F, transmission F), and its top engine/transmission problem is stalling / loss of power, which a recall covers. The 2007 grades a D overall (engine C-, transmission F), and its top engine/transmission problem is stalling / loss of power.

Is the Dodge Charger transmission reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Dodge Charger transmission a B+ (above-average reliability). Reliability varies by model year, so grade the specific year you're looking at.

Is the Dodge Charger engine reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Dodge Charger engine an A- (excellent reliability).

Grades are estimates for a make and model across model years, not a specific vehicle. Always get a pre-purchase inspection and check open recalls by VIN.
