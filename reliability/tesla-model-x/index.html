Tesla Model X reliability FAQ

Is the Tesla Model X reliable?

Below-average reliability. The Tesla Model X earns a C- overall reliability grade from GradeMyCar, with a D for its motor and battery and a B- for its drivetrain. It ranks 3rd of 4 electric vehicles we grade. The motor and battery (D) are its weaker area, so check battery health and charging history before buying. Grades are based on 1,356 owner complaints filed with NHTSA for the 2016-2023 model years, adjusted for how many were sold.

How many miles will a Tesla Model X last?

A well-maintained Tesla Model X typically lasts around 200,000 miles, and owners have reported examples reaching 300,000 miles. About 11.5% reach 250,000 miles.

What are the best years for the Tesla Model X?

No Tesla Model X model year stands out as exceptionally reliable in NHTSA owner complaints. The best-graded years are 2018, 2019 and 2021.

What Tesla Model X years should I avoid?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the Tesla Model X years to be most careful with are 2016, 2017 and 2020. The 2016 grades a D overall (motor and battery F, drivetrain C+), and its most common complaint is suspension problems. The 2017 grades a D overall (motor and battery D, drivetrain C+), and its most common complaint is suspension problems.

Is the Tesla Model X drivetrain reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Tesla Model X drivetrain a B- (average reliability). Reliability varies by model year, so grade the specific year you're looking at.

Is the Tesla Model X motor and battery reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Tesla Model X motor and battery a D (poor reliability).

Grades are estimates for a make and model across model years, not a specific vehicle. Always get a pre-purchase inspection and check open recalls by VIN.
