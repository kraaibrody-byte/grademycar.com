Ford Focus reliability FAQ

Is the Ford Focus reliable?

Poor reliability. The Ford Focus earns an F overall reliability grade from GradeMyCar, with an F for its engine and an F for its transmission. It ranks 14th of 14 compact cars we grade. Both its engine (F) and transmission (F) grade poorly, so a pre-purchase inspection by a mechanic is a must. Grades are based on 8,733 owner complaints filed with NHTSA for the 2013-2018 model years, adjusted for how many were sold.

How many miles will a Ford Focus last?

A well-maintained Ford Focus typically lasts around 180,000 miles, and owners have reported examples reaching 240,000 miles. About 8.4% reach 250,000 miles.

What are the best years for the Ford Focus?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the most reliable Ford Focus model years are 2006, 2008 and 2011.

What Ford Focus years should I avoid?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the Ford Focus years to be most careful with are 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017 and 2018. The 2012 grades an F overall (engine D, transmission F), and its most common complaint is transmission slipping / rough shifting. The 2013 grades an F overall (engine C-, transmission F), and its most common complaint is transmission slipping / rough shifting, which a recall covers.

Is the Ford Focus transmission reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Ford Focus transmission an F (poor reliability). Reliability varies by model year, so grade the specific year you're looking at.

Is the Ford Focus engine reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Ford Focus engine an F (poor reliability).

Grades are estimates for a make and model across model years, not a specific vehicle. Always get a pre-purchase inspection and check open recalls by VIN.
