Tesla Model S reliability FAQ

Is the Tesla Model S reliable?

Below-average reliability. The Tesla Model S earns a C- overall reliability grade from GradeMyCar, with a D for its motor and battery and a B- for its drivetrain. It ranks 3rd of 4 electric vehicles we grade. The motor and battery (D) are its weaker area, so check battery health and charging history before buying. Grades are based on 3,125 owner complaints filed with NHTSA for the 2013-2023 model years, adjusted for how many were sold.

How many miles will a Tesla Model S last?

A well-maintained Tesla Model S typically lasts around 220,000 miles, and owners have reported examples reaching 350,000 miles. About 12.8% reach 250,000 miles.

What are the best years for the Tesla Model S?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the most reliable Tesla Model S model years are 2019.

What Tesla Model S years should I avoid?

Based on NHTSA owner complaints, the Tesla Model S years to be most careful with are 2013, 2014, 2015 and 2016. The 2013 grades an F overall (motor and battery F, drivetrain D), and its top engine/transmission problem is stalling / loss of power, which a recall covers. The 2014 grades an F overall (motor and battery F, drivetrain D), and its top engine/transmission problem is stalling / loss of power, which a recall covers.

Is the Tesla Model S drivetrain reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Tesla Model S drivetrain a B- (average reliability). Reliability varies by model year, so grade the specific year you're looking at.

Is the Tesla Model S motor and battery reliable?

GradeMyCar grades the Tesla Model S motor and battery a D (poor reliability).

Grades are estimates for a make and model across model years, not a specific vehicle. Always get a pre-purchase inspection and check open recalls by VIN.
